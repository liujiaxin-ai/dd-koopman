\title{DD-Koopman: Exactness on a Budget for Compact Multi-Step CSI Prediction}

\name{Jiaxin Liu$^{1,\ast}$, Ting Wu$^{1,\ast}$}
\address{$^{1}$Nanjing University \quad $^{\ast}$Corresponding authors: T. Wu, J. Liu}

\begin{document}
\ninept
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multi-step channel state information (CSI) prediction is usually cast as
sequence-to-sequence learning, yet a specular channel's future is fixed by
a few parameters: state estimation followed by a small linear
extrapolation, and the budget belongs in the estimator. DD-Koopman builds
that split -- input-side estimation with a conditional spectral readout
over a parameter-free delay--Doppler lift -- in 173{,}190 parameters,
reaches 0.1402 mean NMSE against the 21.9M-parameter reference's 0.1246 at
126.5$\times$ fewer parameters and 4.98$\times$ lower latency, and improves
on a 432-setting unseen-propagation slice across three seeds. Exact
prediction of a $K$-mode exponential support requires $PV=Q$: at full grid
support the exact transfer is unique with zero degrees of freedom and
reduces to periodic continuation, while covering $K$ grid modes buys
$2T(N{-}K)$ degrees of freedom at a minimum white-noise gain of $KT/N$; off
grid, the worst-case mixture error is the budget times the spectral
defect, certified from a finite scan. The measurements close the loop: the
zero-parameter exact transfer scores 3.2883 against the learned model's
0.1402, a fitted readout on the same 16 grid bins removes most of that
error, and the largest measured degradation is estimator-side, not
transfer-side.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Channel prediction, CSI, operator learning, delay--Doppler, parameter efficiency
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The CSI prediction task is to forecast the next four channel states from
sixteen historical observations on a
$4{\times}4{\times}2$ MIMO array over 300 subcarriers. The
CSI-4CAST benchmark~\cite{csi4cast_2025} standardises the
splits and the metric and publishes a reference model that reaches a mean
normalised mean square error (NMSE) of 0.1246 on its official 162-setting
TDD-regular grid, at the cost of 21{,}913,750 parameters. Our harness
reproduces the published
number within $0.105\%$ (the benchmark does not seed its RNG), so every
comparison below runs on the same protocol.

Per-path frequency, radius and delay amplitude fix a specular
channel's future, so the task is short-window identification and
extrapolation rather than sequence modelling. The
analysis below characterizes which transfers are exact, what full-grid
periodic exactness costs on this benchmark, and how badly a transfer
degrades off its support; the
experiments locate the practical bottleneck: the exact transfer itself scores 3.2883 against the learned model's 0.1402, and removing the input-side denoiser costs 27.3\% NMSE.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Exactness as a budget.} We characterize finite-window
spectral extrapolation as a constrained problem: exact prediction of a
$K$-mode exponential support requires $PV=Q$ -- unique with zero
degrees of freedom at full grid support, $2T(N{-}K)$ real degrees of
freedom at $K$ modes with the minimum white-noise gain $KT/N$ attained
by the minimum-norm member $P_0=QV^{\dagger}$ -- and over a compact
off-grid class the worst-case noiseless error is the amplitude budget
times the spectral defect, certifiable from a finite scan.
\item \textbf{A predictor built to the split.} DD-Koopman separates
estimation from extrapolation: a 12{,}768-parameter
conditioned spectral operator carries 7.4\% of the 173{,}190 parameters,
the rest in input processing. The split is testable, and the tests
agree: a grid-pinned retrain matches the reported model, so off-grid
pole placement is not necessary once the estimator and readout are
re-optimized; removal costs under matched 240-epoch controls rank the
roles -- 0.8\% for pole conditioning, 14.3\% for the readout gains'
observation conditioning, 27.3\% for the input denoiser.
\item \textbf{Design dominates capacity, and the analysis is checkable.}
Accuracy stays within 0.1367--0.1423 across a $5.6\times$ parameter scan
(58k--327k) while comparable-capacity architectures remain above 0.18; on
the frozen checkpoints the transfer expansion reproduces the network
output exactly and the finite-scan defect certificate holds on every
evaluated instance. DD-Koopman reaches 0.1402 against the published 0.1246
with 126.5$\times$ fewer parameters and 4.98$\times$ lower measured
latency, and improves mean NMSE on a 432-setting unseen-propagation
slice, each paired CI entirely below zero
(\S\ref{ssec:main}, \S\ref{ssec:shift}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Benchmark and published baselines.}
CSI-4CAST~\cite{csi4cast_2025} provides the training grid, the three test
splits, the harness, and the published reference rows we compare against,
including the language-model predictor LLM4CP~\cite{llm4cp_2024}; neural
channel prediction predates the
benchmark~\cite{oshea2017physical,wen2018csinet,lu2020crnet}. Cross-paper
NMSE is not comparable, so we use the benchmark's published reference rows
and re-implement the Table~\ref{tab:main} models through its harness.

\textbf{Delay-domain representations.}
OTFS~\cite{hadani2017otfs} made the delay--Doppler plane the modulation
domain on the same sparsity. ChannelKAN~\cite{channelkan_2026},
MambaCSP~\cite{mambacsp_2026}, PCGAE-Net~\cite{pcgae_2026} and
CSI-BERT2~\cite{csibert2_2024} embed delay features next to a generic
model. We instead study the extrapolation map itself: its exact
solution set, its noise amplification, and its off-support spectral
defect, with the plane as the space where the operator is diagonal.

\textbf{Dynamics-based and state-space prediction.}
The Koopman programme lifts nonlinear dynamics to linear evolution of
observables~\cite{koopman1931,mezic2005,brunton2017havok}, approximated
by EDMD~\cite{williams2015koopman}, deep
embeddings~\cite{lusch2018koopman} and neural
operators~\cite{lu2021deeponet,li2021fno}. Structured, selective and
gated recurrences~\cite{gu2022s4,smith2023s5,mamba_2023,orvieto2023lru},
with CSI adaptations~\cite{mambacsp_2026,cpmamba_2025}, learn
input-dependent recurrences in latent coordinates. Our operator is
diagonal in an explicit delay--Doppler basis with a closed-form
transfer, and we measure what one frozen model retains under shift.

\textbf{Efficient forecasting backbones.}
PatchTST~\cite{patchtst_2023}, iTransformer~\cite{itransformer_2024},
TimeMixer~\cite{timemixer_2024}, TSMixer~\cite{tsmixer_2023} and
Mamba~\cite{mamba_2023} compactify generic sequence modelling; we port,
retrain and score them under the budgets stated in Table~\ref{tab:main},
alongside the attention, recurrent and linear families they grew
from~\cite{vaswani2017attention,hochreiter1997lstm,cho2014gru,zeng2023dlinear}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig1_final}
  \caption{The 21.9M-parameter CSI-4CAST reference attaches the
  delay domain to a generic backbone; this work puts the evolution operator
  in that plane (173,190 parameters): (1) residual denoiser on the observed
  history; (2)
  parameter-free IDFT/DFT lift to the delay--Doppler plane;
  (3) pooled context generating the poles $\zeta_m(\cc)$ and gains
  $G_h[m](\cc)$, two residual paths gated from zero re-entering it;
  (4) four-slot prediction.}
  \label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{Lift.} Per antenna, the history is
$\Hm \in \mathbb{C}^{16\times 300}$ (16 slots, 300 subcarriers) and the
target is the next four slots; Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} gives the signal
path. Two parameter-free transforms map the observation into the
working basis:
$a = \mathrm{IDFT}_{\mathrm{sub}}(\Hm)$ converts each slot to its delay
profile (300 taps), and $\Dd = \mathrm{DFT}_{\mathrm{time}}(a)$ converts
along time, giving the delay--Doppler plane. A specular channel concentrates
in a few delay taps and few Doppler modes, so the dynamics live in a
sparse, physically derived coordinate system; the lift has no parameters,
and all 173{,}190 parameters belong to the operator, the residual
denoiser, and the gated correction paths.

\textbf{Input processing (the estimator).}
A small convolutional \emph{residual denoiser} acts on the input history
before the lift through a zero-initialised head, starting as the
identity. Two residual paths then re-enter the \emph{same}
operator, each contributing a gated difference of two operator
evaluations, $\hat y=\mathrm{base}+g_1(\mathrm{op}(\mathrm{mix}(x))-
\mathrm{base})+g_2(\mathrm{op}(\mathrm{res}(x))-\mathrm{base})$:
$\mathrm{mix}$ applies per-tap adaptive reweighting (initialised to one)
and a temporal convolution (initialised to zero); $\mathrm{res}$ is the
unexplained part of a learned delay-profile split, a delay-domain
analogue of series decomposition~\cite{wu2021autoformer}. Their
scalar gates start at zero, so the sum initialises to exactly the base;
each antenna is processed independently with shared weights.
The parameter inventory in
\S\ref{ssec:alloc} confirms the estimator split.

\textbf{Conditioned spectral transfer.}
For horizon $h \in \{1,\dots,4\}$ (target slot $15{+}h$), the base
prediction extrapolates each mode exponentially:
\begin{equation}
\hat a[h,l] \;=\; \sum_{m} G_h[m](\cc)\; \Dd[m,l]\; \zeta_m(\cc)^{\,h},
\label{eq:operator}
\end{equation}
where $m$ indexes the 16 Doppler bins, $l$ the 300 delay taps, and applying
$\mathrm{DFT}_{\mathrm{sub}}$ to $\hat a$ reconstructs the predicted
frequency-domain CSI. The poles are
$\zeta_m(\cc) = r_m(\cc)\,e^{j2\pi(m+\varepsilon_m(\cc))/16}$ with total
offset $\varepsilon_m(\cc) = \delta_m + \Delta_m(\cc)$: a learned static
offset $\delta_m$ plus an observation-conditioned shift $\Delta_m(\cc)$,
each in $(-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, so $\varepsilon_m(\cc)$ places every pole
continuously between the DFT bins. Both the radii $r_m(\cc) \in (0,1)$
and the offsets depend on the observation through a context $\cc$ that
summarises the observed Doppler power averaged over delay taps; the core
$K_c = \mathrm{diag}(\zeta_m(\cc))$ is held fixed across the four
horizons. The gains $G_h[m](\cc)$ are complex, indexed by horizon and
mode, shared across delay taps, and modulated per observation from the
same context; they calibrate the finite-window readout, while the
semigroup law $K_c^{\,h+k}=K_c^{\,h}K_c^{\,k}$, non-expansive in every
power ($\|K_c^{\,h}\|_2\le 1$), belongs to $K_c$ alone. The reported
model initializes its offsets on the grid ($\delta_m$, $\Delta_m(\cc)$
start at zero) and applies the ridge least-squares warm start.

\textbf{Effective transfer and notation.} $N{=}16$ history slots,
$T{=}4$ horizons, $L{=}300$ delay taps; $X\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times L}$ and
$Y,\widehat Y\in\mathbb{C}^{T\times L}$ in delay coordinates;
$P\in\mathbb{C}^{T\times N}$ an effective temporal transfer; $z$ an
exponential factor with atoms
$v_N(z)=(1,z,\ldots,z^{N-1})^{\mathrm T}$,
$u_T(z)=(z^{N},\ldots,z^{N+T-1})^{\mathrm T}$; $\mathcal Z$ a designated
factor set; $\dagger$ the pseudoinverse, $\mathrm{H}$ the conjugate
transpose. All branches re-enter one operator, so each
forward pass acts on its branch input through the same conditional
form: $P_j=W_jF_N$ with $(W_j)_{hm}=G_{hm}(\cc)\,\zeta_m(\cc)^h$, and with
gates $\alpha=(1{-}g_1{-}g_2,\,g_1,\,g_2)$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat Y=P_XX+R_X,\quad
P_X=\textstyle\sum_j\alpha_jP_j,\;\;
R_X=\sum_j\alpha_jP_j(A_j{-}X),
\label{eq:expansion}
\end{equation}
holds exactly, both objects computed from the same pass. Here
$A_0,A_1,A_2$ are the delay-domain histories reaching the base,
reweighting/mixer, and decomposition-residual branches, and each
$P_j$ is recomputed from its branch context, $P_j=P(A_j)$: the
denoiser, reweighting and decomposition shape both $P_X$ and
$R_X$. $P_X$ carries $T{\times}N$ complex coefficients, independent
of the parameter count.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (exactness and its price).} For $K\le N$ distinct
factors, let $V=[v_N(z_k)]$, $Q=[u_T(z_k)]$. A fixed $P$ predicts every
pair $VB\mapsto QB$ exactly iff $PV=Q$; the solution set has real
dimension $2T(N{-}K)$, and $P_0=QV^{\dagger}$ is its unique
minimum-Frobenius-norm member. At full grid support ($K{=}N$ unit roots)
$Q=[I_T\;0]V$, so the solution is unique with zero degrees of freedom
and $P_0=[I_T\;0]$: periodic continuation, $\widehat Y=X[0{:}T]$. On a
grid support the output-noise gain
$G(P)=\mathbb{E}\|Pe\|_2^2/\sigma^2=\|P\|_F^2$ for
$e\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^2I_N)$ is minimized among exact transfers by
$P_0$, with $G(P_0)=KT/N$; off grid
$\operatorname{tr}[Q(V^{\mathrm H}V)^{-1}Q^{\mathrm H}]$ replaces it
\cite{golub2013matrix}.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (spectral-defect bound).} Let
$d_P(z)=Pv_N(z)-u_T(z)$ and
$\delta_{\mathcal Z}(P)=\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\|d_P(z)\|_2$ over compact
$\mathcal Z$. Over finite mixtures
$S=\sum_kv_N(z_k)b_k^{\mathrm T}$,
$Y=\sum_ku_T(z_k)b_k^{\mathrm T}$ with $z_k\in\mathcal Z$ and
$\sum_k\|b_k\|_2\le M$, the worst
noiseless error of a fixed $P$ is exactly $M\,\delta_{\mathcal Z}(P)$,
attained at a single atom; and the implemented predictor, with $P_X$ and
$R_X$ fixed by the observed $X$, obeys
\begin{equation}
\|\widehat Y-Y\|_F\;\le\;M\,\delta_{\mathcal Z}(P_X)
+\epsilon\|P_X\|_2+\|R_X\|_F
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
whenever $\|X-S\|_F\le\epsilon$: a per-input diagnostic separating
model-class defect, input perturbation, and correction magnitude;
which modules help is established by the matched ablations of
\S\ref{ssec:ablations}.

\textbf{Corollary 1 (finite-scan certificate).} Let
$\mathcal G$ be finite with
$\mathcal Z\cup\mathcal G\subset\{z:|z|\le\rho\}$ and every
$z\in\mathcal Z$ lie within $\eta$ of some $g\in\mathcal G$. With
$L_v(\rho)=\big(\sum_{p=1}^{N-1}p^2\rho^{2p-2}\big)^{1/2}$ and
$L_u(\rho)=\big(\sum_{p=N}^{N+T-1}p^2\rho^{2p-2}\big)^{1/2}$,
$\delta_{\mathcal Z}(P)\le\widehat\delta_{\mathcal G}(P)
+\eta(\|P\|_2L_v(\rho) + L_u(\rho))$, where $\widehat\delta_{\mathcal G}$
is the grid maximum: a finite scan certifies the continuous defect.

\emph{Proof sketch.} \eqref{eq:expansion} adds and subtracts $X$
branchwise; $V$ has full column rank (Vandermonde), so every exact $P$
is $P_0+Z(I{-}VV^{\dagger})$, orthogonal to $P_0$
($P_0(I{-}VV^{\dagger})=0$) with arbitrary $Z$; the bound expands
the mixture into outer products and attains it at one atom; the
certificate uses $|z^p-g^p|\le p\rho^{p-1}|z-g|$.~$\blacksquare$

On the frozen checkpoints these objects are measurable: the expansion
reproduces the network's delay output to floating-point precision
(relative residual $3\times10^{-15}$ in float64; the float32 residual
$1.97\times10^{-6}$ is the model's own forward arithmetic). The
implementation satisfies the finite-scan inequality in all 311{,}040
evaluated instances -- 257 unit-circle angles, $\rho{=}1$,
$\eta{=}\pi/256$; the mixture bound \eqref{eq:bound} holds with zero
violations in 10{,}800 synthetic trials, with median bound-to-true
ratio 3.84$\times$ (5th--95th percentile 2.37--6.24$\times$).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{ssec:setup}

The training grid has 27 subsets (3 channel models $\times$ 3 delay spreads
$\times$ 3 speeds), 24{,}300 training and 2{,}700 validation samples. The
test splits are the \emph{regular} split (162 settings: channel model $\times$
delay spread $\times$ speed $\times$ six SNRs) and the \emph{robustness}
split (486 settings: phase/burst noise and packet drops over the 27
regular combinations). The
\emph{generalization} slice has 432 settings over 72 combinations drawn
from channel models B and E, with delay spreads 50/200/400\,ns and speeds
3--42\,m/s, all absent from training; the metric is the benchmark's mean
NMSE over the four horizons. For paired statistics we average NMSE over the six SNRs in each
channel--delay--speed cell and bootstrap paired cell differences
(5{,}000 resamples, 95\% CI); intervals describe variation across
cells. Training uses batch 32, Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} at
$5{\cdot}10^{-4}$ with a plateau scheduler and early stopping (600 epochs reported, 240 for the ablation table), three seeds.



\subsection{Where the parameters go}
\label{ssec:alloc}

The implementation follows the split: the conditioned spectral operator
holds 12{,}768 parameters (7.4\%), per-tap reweighting 154{,}328 (89.1\%),
the temporal mixer 4{,}200, the residual denoiser 1{,}590, the delay
decomposition 300, and four scalar gates, for 173{,}190 in total.
Table~\ref{tab:main} places this split on the accuracy--efficiency plane.

\subsection{The price of exactness}
\label{ssec:price}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.5\columnwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_defect.pdf}
\caption{Off-grid response of the learned transfer (seed 42,
validation split): radius shows the per-angle median defect
$\|d_P(z)\|_2$, grey per-cell medians, blue 5th--95th percentile
bands, dots the 16 DFT bin phases -- reference phases, not
zero-defect constraints.}
\label{fig:defect}
\end{wrapfigure}
Proposition~1's full-grid exact transfer scores end to end without a
fitted parameter: it is periodic continuation, reaching 3.2883 on the
regular split -- the worst row in Table~\ref{tab:main} -- and 2.7558
and 2.7518 on the shifted splits. Raising the support from the
adaptive top-1 bin (1.3771) to the full grid degrades monotonically
across $K\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}$, consistent with truncation suppressing noisy
modes. Staying inside the same 16-bin representation but replacing
periodic exactness by a ridge least-squares fit of the same
$4{\times}16$ gains -- the model's own warm start, a 256-sample fit --
reaches 1.2504 (1.1366 and 1.4767 shifted), and fitting on all
24{,}300 training samples reaches 0.8173: within this fixed-grid
comparison, the exactness constraint accounts for most of the gap
to the fitted readout; the ablations below assess the rest. The harness's $K{=}16$ transfer matches the
analytic continuation to float32 precision ($1.7\times10^{-6}$).

Pinning the poles to the grid and retraining recovers the reported
model: 0.1401 against 0.1402 on the regular split and 0.2688 against
0.2716 on the generalization slice (three seeds), and the fix holds
on the 486-setting robustness split (0.1775 against 0.1761). Off-grid
placement is thus not necessary after re-optimization; the fitted
checkpoints still use the freedom, with 89\%
of trained poles more than 0.05 bins off grid and all 16 modes moving
across scenes. Figure~\ref{fig:defect} measures the learned
transfer's off-grid response over the unit circle: the defect falls
to 2.10 at $z{=}1$, peaks at 7.1 in the mid-band, and its per-cell
peak lies between 4.1 and 8.3 in every one of the 27 propagation
cells: the median defect stays elevated over a broad mid-band rather
than being driven by a single angle.

Snapping the offsets of an already-fitted checkpoint, every weight
frozen, moves the fitted transfer outside its calibrated region. Setting $\varepsilon_m(\cc) = 0$ degrades the
official grid from 0.1385 to 0.1923 ($+38.8\%$) with all 162 settings
worse, every constant global offset is worse still, so the gain is
per-mode, per-sample placement, and the penalty rises monotonically over
horizons. The same
intervention degrades all 1{,}728 settings across the four splits
($+17.7\%$ to $+38.8\%$), and on every shifted slice it pushes the
operator below the published model ($+0.0288$ to $+0.0691$, all CIs
entirely above zero).

\textbf{Module factorial.} Table~\ref{tab:factorial} runs the full $2^3$
loss factorial. The residual denoiser is the dominant single effect
($+31.7\%$ average main effect across the four flip pairs), and removing
the denoiser together with either other module costs more than the sum
of the parts: the components complement rather than duplicate. A squared FreDF-style spectral term~\cite{fredf_2025} reweights the same
regression by Parseval's identity, yet every matched comparison trains worse with it (two seeds
at 600 epochs, one at 240); we drop it.

On the generalization slice the same
factorial attributes the shift advantage to the residual denoiser
($+20.9\%$ without it, 95\% CI $[+0.0431,+0.0713]$) and secondarily
to the delay decomposition ($+4.0\%$); removing the pole conditioning
slightly helps ($-0.9\%$).

\textbf{Gain conditioning.} Freezing the readout gains' per-observation modulation to per-mode
constants, under the factorial protocol (240 epochs, seed 42), costs
0.1584 against
0.1386 for a same-environment control arm -- $+14.3\%$, an order of
magnitude above pole conditioning ($+0.8\%$); the control lands next
to the factorial reference 0.1397, so the gap is the intervention.
Under these matched controls, gain conditioning carries the larger
marginal effect; the frozen-checkpoint snap above shows the trained
poles still matter to their jointly fitted configuration.

The transfer applied at each pass is only $T{\times}N$; the hard part is
inferring it from a noisy finite history. The tested bottleneck sits
there: input processing costs 27.3\% when removed, gain conditioning
14.3\%, pole conditioning 0.8\%, and a $5.6\times$ parameter scan
moves accuracy by less than 0.006.

Shrinking the published architecture to 91{,}410 parameters yields 0.1807
at convergence, still 1.29$\times$ our mean; a transformer sized to match
our parameters reaches 0.2106. The MambaCSP port (100 epochs, validation
plateau) remains behind the reported model on all three splits (0.1468,
0.2796 and 0.1805) at 5.1$\times$ our parameters; the remaining ports trail
further (GRU 0.3239, TCN 0.8353, DLinear 0.7941, MLP 0.3143, Mamba 0.3011).
Within our own architecture, accuracy is width-insensitive (0.1367--0.1423
across a $5.6\times$, 58k--327k parameter scan), while halving the
training windows costs about 17\% NMSE: over the tested range,
performance is more sensitive to data than to width.

Table~
\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~
\ref{fig:pareto} give the trade-off: our
three-seed mean NMSE is 0.1402 against the published model's 0.1246 (the
seeds score 0.1385, 0.1442 and 0.1378); the per-seed paired differences
over the 27 channel cells are $+0.0139$, $+0.0196$ and $+0.0132$, each CI
entirely above zero.

The classical predictors bound what fixed assumptions deliver: path-delay
collapses from 0.2300 to 0.3231 under shift, autoregressive (0.7874) and
Wiener (0.8236) degrade to 1.0469 and 1.0862 against the released
checkpoint's 0.2872, and the adaptive top-1 DFT-grid extrapolator reaches
1.3771, 1.2465 and 1.2790.

The reported model carries 173{,}190 parameters and 6{,}514{,}464 MACs per
antenna sample against the published 21{,}913,750 and 804{,}901,376:
126.5$\times$ and 123.6$\times$ ours, counted over matmul and convolution
only; the parameter-free DFT/IDFT lift and elementwise products are
excluded. Forward latency (RTX 4090 D, batch 32, fp32, 12 repeats) is
$2.24\pm0.05$\,ms against $11.15\pm0.02$\,ms per scene: 4.98$\times$
faster. With MRT beamforming the released checkpoint keeps more of the
perfect-CSI rate (2.06\% vs.\ 2.70\% loss, paired $+0.64$\,pp).

\subsection{Behaviour under distribution shift}
\label{ssec:shift}

On the two shifted splits the three frozen checkpoints hold: the
432-setting generalization slice gives 0.2685, 0.2767 and 0.2695 against
the released checkpoint's 0.2872. Each paired difference has a 95\% CI
entirely below zero ($-0.0187$, $-0.0105$, $-0.0177$), and the three-seed
mean difference is $-0.0156$ $[-0.0250,-0.0070]$ over 72 channel cells
(5{,}000-resample bootstrap). The operator leads on model B
($-0.0400$, 95\% CI $[-0.0531,-0.0271]$) while on model E the released
checkpoint stays ahead ($+0.0088$ $[+0.0046,+0.0132]$) at an order of
magnitude smaller NMSE. Mobility holds: below 10\,m/s the three-seed
difference is $-0.0204$ $[-0.0378,-0.0057]$; above 30\,m/s they are
indistinguishable ($+0.0001$, CI $[-0.0180,+0.0169]$).

The shrunk architecture (0.3039), capacity-matched transformer (0.3427)
and with-loss variant (0.2951) all trail on this slice; the MambaCSP
port (0.2796) is not separated (CI $[-0.0178,+0.0019]$).

Across the 486-setting robustness split the released checkpoint leads:
0.1623 against 0.1772, 0.1735 and 0.1777.

On a second shift axis (A, C and D outside their training delay-spread
and speed ranges; TDD only) the operator stays ahead on the mean (0.2031
against 0.2182, $-0.0151$, 95\% CI $[-0.0256,-0.0054]$), with the gain
concentrated at 400\,ns. Consistent with the estimator-based
interpretation, the advantage is largest where the pooled Doppler
context averages over many taps rather than few; the same
holds on the independent B/E
axis ($-0.0367$ at 400\,ns, CI $[-0.0549,-0.0189]$; absent at 50\,ns).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

A multi-step CSI predictor is an estimator followed by a small linear
transfer, and exactness in that transfer is a constrained resource. On this benchmark,
full-grid periodic exactness scores 3.2883 against the learned model's
0.1402 and a fitted readout on the same 16 bins removes most of that
error; removal costs rank 27.3\% (input denoiser), 14.3\% (gains) and
0.8\% (poles) under matched controls. DD-Koopman holds a three-seed
unseen-propagation advantage at
126.5$\times$ fewer parameters and 4.98$\times$ lower latency.
